Faults I Get Called Out To

Most electrical emergencies come down to a handful of causes. Here is what I turn up to most often across the Coast.

🔌

No Power at All

Whole-house outages that are not the network’s fault — usually a main switch, a failed RCD, or a fault on the incoming supply.

🏚️

Half the House Is Dead

One circuit down while the rest works. Almost always a tripped breaker hiding a genuine fault further down the line.

⚠️

Safety Switch Keeps Tripping

An RCD that will not stay on is doing its job — something is leaking to earth. I isolate the circuit and find what is causing it.

🔥

Burning Smell or Scorching

Burnt insulation has a smell you do not forget. Switch it off at the board and call — this one does not wait until morning.

✨

Sparking Powerpoints

Arcing, browning around the outlet, or a plug that is hot to touch. Usually a loose connection, and a genuine fire risk.

🌩️

Storm Damage

Lightning strikes, water in the meter box, or a fallen branch through the service line. Make-safe first, repairs after.

🚿

No Hot Water

Often a failed element or a tripped circuit rather than a dead tank. Worth checking before you replace the whole unit.

💡

Lights Out or Flickering

Flickering across multiple rooms usually points to a loose neutral — worth taking seriously rather than living with.

🗄️

Switchboard Faults

Buzzing, heat, or ceramic fuses that keep blowing. Older boards on the Coast are a common source of trouble.

What to Do Before I Get There

A few minutes of the right thing can stop a fault becoming a fire — and it often helps me find it faster once I arrive.

🛑

If You Can Smell Burning or See Sparks

Turn the main switch off at the switchboard. Do not try to work out which circuit it is first — kill the lot, then call. Unplug whatever was running at the time if you can do it safely, and leave it unplugged. Do not use that powerpoint again until it has been looked at, even if everything seems to go back to normal. A connection that has arced once will arc again, usually worse.

🔦

If the Safety Switch Will Not Reset

Unplug everything on that circuit, then try resetting once. If it holds, plug appliances back in one at a time — the one that trips it is your culprit, and you have just saved me an hour. If it still will not reset with everything unplugged, the fault is in the wiring itself. Leave it off and give me a ring. Do not keep resetting it over and over; you are not fixing anything and you are stressing the device that is protecting you.
