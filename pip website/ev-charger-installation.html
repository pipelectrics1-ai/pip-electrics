What Actually Drives the Price

Most of the cost is not the charger — it is how far it sits from your switchboard and what that board can currently handle.

📏

Cable Run Length

The distance from switchboard to charger is the single biggest variable. A garage next to the meter box is a very different job to a carport at the far end of the block.

🗄️

Switchboard Capacity

Some boards have room and spare capacity. Others need work first — which is worth knowing before you order the charger, not after.

🧱

How the Cable Gets There

Through a roof space is straightforward. Under a slab, through brick, or across a driveway is not. Access is what makes or breaks the quote.

⚖️

Single or Three Phase

Three phase opens up faster charging. Most Coast homes are single phase, which is perfectly fine for overnight charging.

🎛️

Load Management

Where supply is tight, the charger can be set to back off when the house is drawing heavily, rather than upgrading the whole supply.

🔌

Charger Choice

Happy to supply and install, or to install a unit you have bought yourself. I will tell you honestly if the one you have picked is a poor fit.

Why the Garage Powerpoint Is Not the Answer

Trickle charging from a standard outlet is the default for a lot of new EV owners. It works, but it asks something of your wiring that it was never designed for.

🕐

Hours of Continuous Load

A kettle draws hard for two minutes. A car draws steadily for eight to twelve hours, night after night. Standard powerpoints and the circuits behind them were sized for intermittent use, and a marginal connection that would never show up under normal load will heat up under a sustained one. This is the single most common cause of the melted outlets I get called out to look at.

🔗

Everything Else on the Same Circuit

Garage powerpoints are rarely on their own circuit. They usually share with the laundry, the outdoor points, or half the back of the house. Add a car to that and you are running close to the limit for hours at a time. A dedicated circuit means the charger is not competing with anything else, and a fault on the charger does not take out the rest of your house.
