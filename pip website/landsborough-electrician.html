The Landsborough Wiring Reality

Why Landsborough Homes
Need a Full Rewire

Landsborough is one of the oldest townships on the Sunshine Coast. Its housing stock spans the 1940s through to the 1970s — an era when wiring standards, materials and protection methods were completely different. What was compliant then is a serious hazard now.

🔥

Rubber & Cloth Wiring — A Silent Hazard

Landsborough homes from the 1940s to 1970s were wired with rubber-sheathed and cloth-covered cables. These materials have a finite safe service life — and in most cases, that life expired decades ago. The insulation dries out, becomes brittle, and cracks. Inside a wall, this is invisible until it causes a fault, an arc, or a fire.

  • Rubber insulation cracks and crumbles when disturbed during renovation
  • Cloth-covered wiring loses its protective properties entirely over time
  • Exposed copper conductor can contact metal cable clips, nails or pipes
  • Arcing from degraded insulation is one of the leading causes of house fires
  • Once exposed during a renovation, this wiring cannot legally be left in place
⚡

No Safety Switches — No Protection

Homes built before the 1990s were not required to have residual current devices (RCDs), commonly known as safety switches. Without them, there is no automatic protection against electric shock from a faulty appliance, damaged cord, or compromised wiring. In a full rewire, we install a modern switchboard with RCDs protecting every circuit — it's not optional, and it's not an add-on.

  • Single-wire earth (SWE) systems provide no path for fault current
  • No RCD means no protection if you touch a live conductor
  • Old ceramic fuse boards can't accommodate modern safety devices
  • New QLD regulations require safety switches on all circuits
  • We install RCDs on every circuit — power, lighting, and wet areas
A new switchboard with RCDs on every circuit — the standard outcome of a full rewire in a Landsborough home. The old ceramic fuse board and SWE wiring are gone entirely.
