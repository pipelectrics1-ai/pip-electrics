A Common Nambour Problem

Ceramic Fuse Boxes Are Still Common
in Nambour Homes — And They're a Problem

Nambour is one of the Sunshine Coast's oldest established towns. A significant portion of the housing stock — particularly in the older residential streets — was built before 1980, and many of these homes still have the original ceramic fuse box in the switchboard. We see this constantly. It's one of the most common things we find when we attend a Nambour property for any electrical job.

🔥
Ceramic Fuse Boxes — Fire Risk, No Protection, Flagged by Banks & Insurers
Ceramic fuses were the standard before modern circuit breakers existed. The problem: they provide no RCD (safety switch) protection — meaning a fault to earth won't trip the circuit. They can't limit current precisely, making them a fire risk when a wire fault develops. And increasingly, banks flagging them during property valuations and insurers querying them at renewal. If your Nambour home has a ceramic fuse box, you have no trip protection for shock or fire — and you may not know it until something goes wrong.
⚠️ Common in pre-1980 Nambour homes
🏠
Modern Power Demand — Old Wiring Can't Cope
Nambour's older homes were wired for a world without air conditioning, induction cooktops, EV chargers, and multiple large appliances running at once. The wiring gauge and the fuse ratings in ceramic boards simply weren't designed for modern loads. Adding a split system or a new kitchen to an older Nambour home without upgrading the switchboard first is asking for chronic tripping, overloaded circuits, or a fire. We upgrade the switchboard and confirm the wiring is adequate for your current needs — all in one visit.
