Replacing Old Alarms

Why Interconnected Is the Whole Point

The change from standalone alarms to interconnected ones is not paperwork for the sake of it. It is the part that actually buys you time.

🔗

One Goes Off, They All Go Off

A standalone alarm in the kitchen is no use to someone asleep at the other end of the house with the door shut and the aircon running. Interconnected alarms all sound together, so a fire that starts in the living room wakes the person in the back bedroom at the same moment. Modern alarms interconnect wirelessly, which means it can usually be done without pulling ceilings apart to run cable between them.

💨

Photoelectric, Not Ionisation

The two alarm types respond to different things. Photoelectric alarms detect the slow, smouldering smoke that most fatal house fires start with — a cigarette in a lounge, wiring behind a wall, something left on a stovetop. They are also far less prone to the nuisance alarms that make people take the battery out, which is how a lot of homes end up with no working alarm at all.
